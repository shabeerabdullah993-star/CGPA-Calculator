What is CGPA?

CGPA, or Cumulative Grade Point Average, is a single number that summarizes a student's academic performance across every semester completed so far. Unlike a semester GPA, which only reflects one term, CGPA is weighted across all your credit hours to date — making it the figure most universities and employers look at first.

Most institutions use a 4.0 scale, where an A is worth 4.00 points and an F is worth 0.00, with plus and minus grades stepping in between. This calculator follows that standard scale, so the number it gives you matches what appears on most transcripts.

How CGPA is calculated

Each course contributes "quality points," found by multiplying its credit hours by its grade point. A semester's GPA is the sum of its courses' quality points divided by its total credit hours. CGPA repeats that same calculation, but across every semester entered — total quality points earned, divided by total credit hours attempted.

Example

A 3-credit course with a B (3.00) earns 9.00 quality points. Add a 4-credit A (4.00) course and you have 16.00 more — 25.00 total quality points over 7 credit hours, for a 3.57 GPA that term.

Frequently asked questions

Is this calculator accurate for my university?

Most universities worldwide use a standard 4.0 scale with the same plus/minus steps used here. If your institution uses a different scale (such as a 5.0 or percentage-based system), the totals here won't map directly — check your registrar's grading policy first.

Does this save or share my data?

No. Every calculation happens locally in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server or stored anywhere — refreshing the page clears it.

What's the difference between GPA and CGPA?

GPA usually refers to a single semester's average, while CGPA is cumulative — it factors in every semester you've completed. This tool shows both: a GPA per semester, and one overall CGPA at the top.

Can I add courses from previous semesters I've already finished?

Yes — add a semester for each term you've completed, with its actual courses, hours, and grades. The CGPA figure at the top will reflect all of them combined.
